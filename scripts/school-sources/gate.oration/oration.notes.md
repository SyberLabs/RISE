# Translator's notes: Oratio de hominis dignitate

Keyed by paragraph (§) and sentence number. One line each: the uncertainty, then the choice made.
These are the translator's doubts, not a check of fidelity; the owner's reading against the Latin is the check.

## Conventions applied throughout

- Names: the person Pico names is kept (Mercury, not Hermes; Abdala, Evantes, Maumeth as given). Where English has a settled form of the same Latin name the spelling is normalized (Timeus > Timaeus, Dyonisius > Dionysius, Hierusalem > Jerusalem, Abraam > Abraham, Hieremias > Jeremiah, Sculapius > Aesculapius, Proculus > Proclus, Damascus > Damascius, Calipsus > Calypso, Protheus > Proteus, Ulixes > Ulysses, Egidius > Giles, Saraph/Seraphin > Seraph/Seraphim). Obscure or garbled forms are kept as printed (Xamolsis, Xalmosis, Abbaris, Oromasius, Aglaophenus, Abumasar, Avenzoar, Alchindus, Hesdras, Iesu Nave). If the owner prefers every name exactly as printed, this list is the set to revert.
- Greek and Hebrew: bracketed in the edition's own Latin-letter transliteration, unchanged, including its case endings (goeteian, mageian, sumpatheian) and its spellings (hasheckinah, dia meson logon).
- Pico's authorial "we" (proposuimus, nostrae infirmitatis) is rendered "I" where it plainly means the speaker alone; "we" is kept where it includes the audience.
- Sentence 213 holds two Latin sentences under one number; the English keeps both under 213.

## Notes by paragraph

- §2 (3): *hymeneum*: "wedding song" or "marriage"; chose "marriage" as the plainer reading of the bond image.
- §2 (3): *stabilis evi et fluxi temporis interstitium*: literally "the interval between"; chose "the meeting-point between".
- §3 (9): *hanc mihi operam condonate*: "grant me this effort" or "indulge me in this"; chose "grant me a kind hearing for this work".
- §4 (11): *excrementarias ac feculentas*: literally "excremental and dreggy"; chose "the dregs and refuse".
- §5 (20): *in cuius manu te posui*: *cuius* taken as referring to *arbitrio* (free choice), not to God; "in whose hand".
- §5 (22): *arbitrarius honorariusque plastes et fictor*: *honorarius* can mean "honoured" or "given as an honour"; chose "free and honoured shaper and molder".
- §6 (26): *e bulga matris*: *bulga* is a leather bag (Lucilius's word for the womb); kept "mother's bag".
- §6 (31): *omnibus antestabit*: "will surpass all" or "stand before all"; chose "stand before all things", keeping the rank image.
- §7 (34): *Asclepius Atheniensis*: the figure is uncertain; kept as printed.
- §8 (36): *reformant* is plural with a singular subject (*theologia*); read as "the Hebrews' secret theology transforms", subject understood as the theologians.
- §8 (40): *fantasiae quasi Calipsus vanis praestigiis*: the illusions are the imagination's, likened to Calypso's; chose "the empty illusions of fantasy, as if by Calypso's".
- §9 (46): *desultoriae*: "leaping from one thing to another"; chose "shifting".
- §10 (48): quotation of Psalm 48 (Vulgate) in indirect form; rendered as direct quotation in first person plural.
- §11 (58): *recto examine*: "with right weighing" kept for the image of judgment as a balance.
- §12 (69): *nodus*: "knot" or "bond"; chose "bond". *preses*: "president" or "guardian"; chose "guardian".
- §14 (76): *ne nobis nostri sufficiant*: literally "lest our own suffice us"; chose "so that we do not rely on ourselves alone".
- §15 (85): *obtorto collo*: literally "with twisted neck", that is, dragged by force; chose "by the throat".
- §15 (87): *artem sermocinalem sive rationariam*: "the art of speech, or of reasoning", read as dialectic.
- §16 (91): *in altum iactari*: "tossed on the open sea" or "flung on high"; chose "tossed upon the deep".
- §17 (93): *multiplicis bruti*: Plato's many-headed beast; chose "the many-formed beast" to keep Pico's word.
- §17 (98): still indirect speech governed by *iubeat* in 97; added "it tells us" to carry that.
- §18 (103): *cuius meditationem*: the philosophy-as-practice-for-death of the Phaedo; chose "rehearsal".
- §19 (104): *a ... fontana plenitudine ... paulo deminutum*: the comparison is with the fullness itself; chose "falls only a little short of".
- §19 (107): *pellicea elementa*: the skin coverings of the tabernacle, read as the elements; chose "the coverings of skin, the elements". Uncertain.
- §20 (110): *februales artes*: from *februa*, rites of purification; chose "cleansing arts".
- §20 (117): *ad modulos* and *ad numerum*: musical terms; chose "to the measures" and "in rhythm".
- §20 (119): *illorum ... huius*: "the one" (past, present, future) and "the other" (the primal beauty); the reference is compressed in the Latin too.
- §21 (123): *cynnus*: read as *cinnus*, a mixture or blended drink; chose "blend".
- §22 (129): *cuius pater ac dux*: *cuius* could refer to Bacchus or to contemplation; chose contemplation.
- §23 (142): *undis Hibericis* and *boreali amussi*: "Iberian waters" and "northern rule" are both obscure; kept literal.
- §23 (145): *ad lineam*: "exactly" or "to the full"; chose "fully".
- §25 (156): *hoc uno nomine*: "on this one count", not "by this one name".
- §26 (166): *illa ita me docente*: read as philosophy now teaching him not to answer for quarrel's sake; the clause is compressed and the reading is uncertain.
- §26 (167): *incognoscamus*: rare; read as "examine, look into".
- §26 (164): *apostolico senatu*: the papal court; kept literal "apostolic senate".
- §28 (175): *spiritum esse, spiritum esse in omnibus*: Job 32:8 as Pico gives it; kept "a spirit in all".
- §29 (182): *exanclandus*: "to be drained" or "endured"; chose "borne".
- §32 (209): *asymboli*: Greek loanword, "without paying one's share"; chose "without our share".
- §32 (210): *ex commentario*: "from a commentary" or "from a notebook"; chose "out of a notebook".
- §35 (219): *ut iactant quidam*: "as some boast" or "as some keep saying"; chose the latter.
- §38 (249): *inter sparsas ... et inter seminatas ... virtutes*: the double *inter* is awkward; read the powers as the object of *evocans*.
- §38 (250): *iunges*: the Greek *iynges* (magical lures); kept the edition's form.
- §39 (255): *circumlatorum*: read as *circulatorum*, peddlers or travelling showmen; chose "peddlers".
- §40 (256): *secretionem*: read as *secretiorem*, "a more secret"; *Iesu Nave* is Joshua son of Nun, kept as printed.
- §41 (266): *ek nou eis noun dia meson logon*: kept the edition's transliteration; rendered by Pico's own gloss.
- §42 (268): *ecclesiae praefectus*: chose "head of the congregation" over "head of the church".
- §44 (281): *pedibus manibusque descendere*: idiom for full agreement; chose "come over, hand and foot".
- §45 (282): *carminibus*: "songs" or "poems"; chose "songs".
- §45 (287): *syrpis*: read as *scirpis*, rush-knots, that is, riddles; chose "tangles".
