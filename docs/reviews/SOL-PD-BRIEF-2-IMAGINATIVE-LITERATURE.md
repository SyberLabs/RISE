# Brief for SOL — second acquisitions pass: the missing literature

*Issued 2026-07-28, correcting the first brief.*

---

## Start here: the first brief was wrong

Your dossier delivered exactly what was asked for. The problem is what
was asked for.

The first brief said: *"Texts are read aloud in the mind, slowly. Prose
that is merely informative reads poorly here. Prose with rhythm,
compression, and authority reads well. Aphorism, dialogue, verse, and
the essay in its older sense all work."*

Every clause of that is true, and together they added up to a
disqualification nobody wrote down. Look at what the Western shelf now
holds:

> Marcus Aurelius · Epictetus · Boethius · Montaigne · Thoreau ·
> Emerson · Rilke · Whitman · Dickinson · Blake · Nietzsche · Julian ·
> the Hermetica

**Thirteen essayists, poets, and philosophers. No epic. No drama. No
novel.** No Homer, no Dante, no Chaucer, no Shakespeare, no Cervantes,
no Milton, no Goethe, no Melville. A canon of Western literature with
its entire imaginative tradition missing.

The failure was mine, not yours: *aphorism and compression* quietly
became *short*, and "long" became a disqualifier that was never stated
and never examined. **Paradise Lost is compressed. The Inferno is
compressed.** They are simply also long.

So: this brief asks for what the first one excluded.

---

## What we need

**Imaginative literature — the works that tell stories and stage
arguments rather than assert them.** Epic, drama, narrative poetry, the
novel, and the great cycles.

The obvious Western absences, which you should treat as a floor rather
than a list:

- **Epic** — Homer, Virgil, Dante, Milton, Ovid, Beowulf, the *Aeneid*'s
  descendants
- **Drama** — the Greek tragedians, Shakespeare, Marlowe, Racine, Ibsen
- **The novel** — Cervantes, Melville, Austen, the Brontës, Dostoevsky,
  Tolstoy, Flaubert, Hawthorne
- **Narrative and lyric cycles** — Chaucer, Spenser, Goethe, the
  troubadours

**And the same absence exists in the other canons, where I cannot judge
the gap.** The Eastern shelf holds nine works and no *Journey to the
West*, no *Tale of Genji*, no *Ramayana* or *Mahabharata* as narrative
rather than as the Gita alone, no Chinese poetry, no Noh drama, no
Persian epic. Tell us what a serious Eastern shelf is missing — that
judgement is yours, not ours.

The Indigenous shelf holds four collected corpora. If there are further
collections that meet the standard of the first four — named tellers,
communities recorded, a collector who learned the language — name them.

## Everything from the first brief still holds

Do not re-derive these; they are unchanged and non-negotiable.

**Rights.** Every text public domain in the US, evidence per EDITION,
never per author. The 1929–1963 renewal trap still applies — check the
Stanford database and cite the record, and read the hits rather than
counting them.

**The dossier format.** title / author / shelf / edition / source /
basis / evidence / why / functions / rhymes / extent / caveats.

**The `why` field.** One or two sentences of judgement in the Archive's
voice, saying something a reader could not have guessed. Not jacket copy.

**Editions.** Where several public-domain translations exist, say which
you recommend and why. Where the only good translation is in copyright,
say so and recommend against ingesting.

## New: the shelves have changed since you last saw them

Two axes now, mirroring how the imagery Collections are organised.

**By tradition** — The Western Canon · The Eastern Canon · Indigenous
Traditions.

**By subject** — Form & Design (the craft books: Vitruvius, Kandinsky,
Dow, Ross, Crane, Dresser).

Each tradition shelf divides in reading order:

| division | holds |
|---|---|
| **Classical** | the founding texts, and commentaries that became texts |
| **Literary** | written in their light — essays, poems, and the argument after |
| **Esoteric** | the hidden reading: what a tradition says when it stops explaining |

**Place every proposal on a shelf AND in a division.** Where a work
could go two ways, say which and why — that reasoning is more useful to
us than the placement.

One live question you are better placed to answer than we are: **the
divisions may not survive this pass.** If forty works of imaginative
literature arrive, "Literary" becomes a bucket holding both Montaigne's
essays and Melville's novel, which are not the same kind of thing. If
the corpus demands a fourth division — Narrative, or Drama, or
Imaginative — propose it, with the argument. We resisted adding
divisions before on the grounds that the parallel across shelves is
itself informative. That argument should lose if the evidence is against
it.

## What we have learned ingesting your first dossier

Practical notes that should shape what you propose.

**Structure is per-EDITION, not per-work.** Five Gutenberg files state
the same three things five different ways. Where an edition has a clean,
machine-detectable division — numbered chapters, consistent headings —
say so. Where it does not, warn us. Westervelt's Hawaiian legends had to
be deferred entirely because the plain-text edition has no detectable
chapter structure, and guessing would have produced sections that are
not the work's own.

**The reading unit is an editorial decision.** Montaigne's three books
parse at ~940,000 characters each — sixty hours a section, a file rather
than a reading. He is read one essay at a time; we split into 102. For
each work you propose, say what the reading unit should be: canto, act,
chapter, book. For Shakespeare, is the play the unit or the scene? For
Dante, the canto or the canticle? You will have better instincts than we
do.

**Beginnings and endings need care.** Editions bury the work between
front matter and apparatus. We now check the first and last section of
every ingest, which caught six defects invisible from the opening lines
— Vitruvius ending on his editor's essay, Boethius running into the
translator's citation list, Ross into an unlabelled paragraph index. If
you know an edition carries a long introduction or a critical apparatus,
say where the work itself starts and stops.

**Length is not a problem.** Payloads load lazily; Montaigne is a 2.8 MB
chunk nobody downloads unless they open him. Do not propose an
abridgement where a complete text exists.

## Scale

**60–100 works.** Weighted toward the Western imaginative absence, which
is the acute gap, but do not neglect the Eastern shelf — that is where
our judgement is weakest and yours matters most.

As before: fewer if that is what survives your standard, and flag
separately anything you want but cannot clear.

---

## The standard, restated

The first brief warned against a long plausible list that dissolves on
inspection. That warning stands.

The new warning is the one this brief exists to correct: **do not let a
stated preference become an unstated exclusion.** We asked for
compression and got a library with no novels. If something about this
brief would similarly narrow the result in a way we have not noticed,
say so before you begin rather than delivering to the letter of it.
