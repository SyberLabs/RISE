# Affect research log

Date: 2026-09-29

This log is the record of what was tried, refused, or left unrun. It is not a claim that the remaining priors are psychological facts.

## What the first version actually does

The runnable text models are `lexical-vad-v1` and `contextual-window-v1`. The first averages a compact in-repo lexicon and ignores negation. The second applies a three-token negation window and an intensifier to the next lexicon word, then a linear map. Both are deterministic and local. The benchmark report is `docs/affect/BENCHMARK.md`, regenerated by `npm run affect:benchmark`.

On the constructed probe "I am not happy.", the baseline stays positive and the window encoder goes negative. That is the evidence the window is doing work. It is not evidence that the magnitudes match any human rater.

Directional checks that held on the archive sample: Paradise Lost's opening woe is negative in valence; Whitman's "I celebrate myself" is positive; the Dostoevsky sentence scores higher tension than the Vitruvius sentence about order. Irony was not solved. Austen and Dickinson are in the report so a person can see the outputs. Dickinson's "kindly" sits next to "Death". The encoder has no figure of speech.

## Rejected

**Generative model as an online judge.** A hosted model asked whether an experience is good would be opaque, off-device, and aimed at the wrong question. The thesis is a shared state, not a grade. The teacher hook exists and records `unavailable` when `AFFECT_TEACHER_URL` is unset. No hosted call was made in this run.

**Published numbers as if they were RISE numbers.** EmoPair-family and other continuous-affect checkpoints were not downloaded and not scored on these passages. The benchmark lists them as not executed. Copying a paper's correlation onto Meditations would be a false measurement.

**MiniLM distillation in this change.** The intended later encoder is a small contextual network, in the MiniLM or small-BERT class, distilled from a teacher and from human preferences, exported to ONNX, with WASM as the reliable runtime and WebGPU only as acceleration. Training it here would require labels. Fitting a probe to scores invented by the same author who wrote the probe would manufacture agreement. `scripts/affect/fit-readout.mjs` exits with status 2 until 24 judgments are marked `annotatorKind: "human"`. It still does not estimate coefficients after that count; the estimator is future work, and the refusal is the current result.

**Hue to sadness.** Blue's swatch is a light blue. The adapter records colorimetric warmth and leaves valence unset. White has no stable hue, so warmth is omitted. That is a scale choice about color temperature, stated as a measurement of the swatch, not a law about mood.

**Emotion labels as the state.** Cue counts can fill an auxiliary distribution. They do not replace the continuous axes, and they are not a trained multilabel head. The loss combination in the brief (regression, ranking, multilabel, distillation, temporal consistency) was not used. There is no teacher tensor and no preference set to weight those terms. Using all of them on synthetic targets would hide which term was doing the work.

**One scalar value.** Fused alignment can look acceptable while pace is not. The 520 WPM case is the demonstration: text, a still image, and quiet audio can sit at low arousal while pace is near the top of the logistic. The result keeps per-modality alignment so the pace can fail on its own. Conflict is zero when the intent marks contrast as deliberate.

**WebGPU as a requirement.** The readout is small enough that the JavaScript core is the runtime. On this machine `npm run affect:bench-runtime` encoded the archive passages in about 0.045 ms each in JavaScript. The exported Gemm, run through `onnxruntime-web` on WASM, matched the JavaScript matrix product to within 3e-8 and took about 0.36 ms per call, slower than the JavaScript path. WebGPU was not requested. The snapshot is `docs/affect/runtime-bench.json`. Prefer the JavaScript core until a larger encoder exists.

**Importing the layer from the player.** Manual configuration must keep working with no model. The module is reached by an explicit import and by `RISE_AFFECT`. It is not on the first-load path.

## Known failures of the window

Negation is not syntax. "Not very happy" works only because the intensifier does not consume the negation window; a longer clause will not. "But" counts as contrast, including Dickinson's "held but just ourselves", which is not contrast. "Fantastic" in Dostoevsky was left out of the lexicon because the word is not stably positive. Irony, understatement, and free indirect style are unmodeled. Low lexicon coverage is a caveat, not a neutral score.

The lexicon itself is an authoring prior for this repository. It is not the NRC VAD lexicon. Those tables were not copied.

## What would change the readout

Human pairwise judgments, stored with `recordJudgment` and never edited, fitted with Bradley–Terry per question. A teacher URL that returns continuous axes for the same passages, stored beside the local outputs rather than averaged into them. Only then a distillation into a small transformer, with the current window encoder kept as the baseline it already is.
