# Jev scene steering: private evaluation

**Status: Human evaluation not run.** The public `/jev-scene-demo` route uses a disclosed fixed Middlemarch preset and makes no live Jev request. It demonstrates the same Shift action available when a live Jev choice admits a multi-scene Gallery. Browser tests mock the provider path; they do not establish live provider reliability or reader enjoyment. Name one pilot owner and record the exact build and dates before inviting anyone.

## Reader job and interaction

A reader asks Jev for a reading, then wants to feel that the experience responds without losing the text. In a Jev-created, multi-scene continuous Gallery, **Shift scene** is one optional action. It brings Jev's next preselected visual scene to the current source position. The reading text, edition, attribution, pace, and Player position stay fixed. No provider request is made during reading. Keep Page, visual-off, reduced-motion, and photosensitivity controls authoritative. If the next scene cannot be prepared, retain the current scene and show a plain failure message. Shift is available only while playing; the Gallery holds its frame during pause. The control says “Next scene selected” after admission because a cue alone does not prove the frame was painted.

Delete chat, new content, automatic intensity, engagement scores, and feeds from this test. One action and one response test reader agency.

## Six-reader test

Invite six consenting readers privately. Explain the interaction, observation, questions, and withdrawal or erasure. Use participant codes; store no names or events without explicit consent. Confirm the reading and visual assets are admitted for private use. Each reader starts one eligible Jev Gallery and may choose Shift scene once. The owner observes whether the next scene visibly differs while text and place remain fixed. Record the source boundary, delays, failed preparation, and Page or visual-off use. Ask, “Did you notice the scene change?” A click does not establish perception.

Afterward ask separately: “Did you finish this reading?”, “Was it worth your time?”, “Did Shift scene make you feel more in control?”, and “Was it distracting?” Preserve each answer and nonresponse. Record the recommendation request count, any in-session provider request, and provider-reported cost for the exact test period. A browser network trace can verify that Shift scene itself made no request; it cannot prove the provider's total bill.

**Pass this small usability gate only if:** at least 5/6 notice the response, 4/6 complete, 4/6 say it was worth their time, 4/6 feel more in control, at most 1/6 calls it distracting, and there are zero unintended text/position changes, safety-control bypasses, or in-session provider requests. Treat missing answers as non-passes. Stop and fix any safety or reading-integrity failure before more sessions. These six invited readers cannot establish retention, broad demand, or a causal improvement over a fixed scene.
