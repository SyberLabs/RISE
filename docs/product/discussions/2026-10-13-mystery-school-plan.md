2026-10-13 · mateo

## Charter

The RISE Mystery School is a free, graded curriculum of public domain texts on consciousness, technology, culture and spirit, read through RISE. It treats reading as initiation: a reader moves through houses and degrees, and every text is chosen because it opened a door that is still open.

The name joins two lineages. The ancient mysteries at Eleusis taught by graded initiation, lesser rites before greater ones, not by lecture. The Renaissance began when recovered texts met a new medium: in 1463 Cosimo de' Medici had Marsilio Ficino set Plato aside to translate the Corpus Hermeticum first, and the printing press carried it across Europe within a decade.

We stand at a similar seam. Machine intelligence is a new medium of readership, and the public domain is a vast recovered library. Neorenaissance here means classical in its sources and avant garde in its medium: old texts, new ways of entering them.

Why public domain: every text can be copied, embedded, re-presented and annotated inside RISE without permission or fee, and handed to anyone. The commons is the school's ground.

## Selection principles

A text enters the canon when it passes the first two tests and at least two of the rest.

1. **Free to carry.** The original and the translation are both public domain in the US. Where only the original is free, the text is admitted as "original language only" until a free translation exists.
2. **Primary, not about.** The source itself, never a summary of it. Commentary is something the school writes, not something it borrows.
3. **Source of a lineage.** It started something that is still alive: a method, a vocabulary, a way of seeing.
4. **Bridge.** It crosses houses. Leibniz belongs to philosophy and computation; Goethe to poetry and biology. Bridges are weighted heavily.
5. **Breadth of civilizations.** Every house reaches beyond Europe where the free record allows.
6. **A threshold in every house.** Difficulty is welcome, but each house has at least one text a newcomer can finish in an evening.
7. **Read against the grain.** Texts with troubled legacies (Futurism, Frazer's colonial frame) may enter with a note, because the school studies how ideas go wrong as well as right.
8. **Clean edition.** A proofed digital text beats raw OCR. Quality of the page is part of the reading.

## The architecture

Everyone enters through the Gate; the seven houses can then be taken in any order, and inside each a reader climbs from Threshold to Arcanum. The houses follow the seven classical planets, a gesture Ficino would recognize: Saturn for contemplation, Mercury for craft and signal, Venus for beauty, Mars for the city and its conflicts.

## How it lives in RISE

Each text becomes one catalog record plus a clean body, so RISE can present it many ways (stream, sequence, constellation) without re-sourcing.

**The record**

| Field | Example |
| --- | --- |
| id | `mercury.lovelace-notes` |
| title, author, date | Sketch of the Analytical Engine, with Notes; Menabrea and Lovelace; 1843 |
| house, degree | Mercury; II |
| edition | Original English, Taylor's *Scientific Memoirs*, vol. 3 |
| translator, translator death year | none |
| rights | US: PD (pre-1931). Life+70: PD |
| source URL | the proofed edition actually used |
| divisions | Notes A to G, as RISE units |
| bridges | Logos (Leibniz), Mind (James) |
| paths | Ghosts in the Machine |

**Rights rules of thumb**

- US: works published in 1930 or earlier are public domain as of January 1, 2026. That covers most of this canon.
- Life+70 countries (EU, UK): both author and translator must have died in 1955 or earlier. Texts that fail this are tagged US only.
- A translation has its own copyright. A free original does not free a modern translation.
- School-written commentary, introductions and new translations are original works; choose an open license for them (CC BY is the simplest).

**Where to source, in order**

1. Standard Ebooks: carefully proofed and typeset, ideal for a reading medium.
2. Project Gutenberg: the widest catalog; if a text is redistributed without its header, the Gutenberg name and license text must be removed.
3. Wikisource: good for shorter works, essays and letters.
4. Internet Archive: original scans, useful to check plates (Haeckel) and verify editions.
5. Perseus: Greek and Latin originals; check each digital edition's license, since some carry share-alike terms.
